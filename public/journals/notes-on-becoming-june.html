June · Entry One
I'm Learning to Exist Without Performing
There is a difference between showing up and putting on a show.

I spent so long curating how I showed up that I forgot what it felt like to just be. Be present without narrating it. Be happy without proving it. Be quiet without it meaning something was wrong.

There was always an audience in my mind. Even when no one was watching, I was still performing — adjusting my reactions, choosing words carefully, making sure the version of me that people saw was acceptable. Lovable. Easy.

But performing is exhausting when it never ends. So I stopped. Not in one big moment. Just in small, repeated choices. Letting myself sit in silence without filling it. Letting my face rest without smiling on command. Letting a moment pass without documenting it or explaining it to anyone.

And something unexpected happened — I started to feel more like myself than I have in years. Not the version I built for others. Not the version that made everyone comfortable. Just me. Unfiltered. Unpolished. Unperformed. It's quieter here. But it's honest.

This is what it feels like to finally stop auditioning for your own life.

June · Entry Two
The Black Coffee Rule
Strip things back to their most honest form.

For a long time, I took my coffee with a little bit of everything. Milk to soften the bitterness, sugar to make it easier to swallow. I dressed it up until it barely tasted like coffee anymore.

And then, one morning, I poured it black. No milk. No sugar. Just the dark, unapologetic reality of it. It was bitter at first — almost too sharp — but then I realized I was finally tasting it for what it actually was.

I call it the black coffee rule. It's the practice of stripping things back to their most honest form. We soften our boundaries so we don't seem difficult. We sweeten our words so we don't seem harsh. We dilute our true desires so they fit neatly into the cups other people hand us.

But what happens when you stop diluting? What happens when you let a moment just be a moment, without needing to dress it up for an audience? It might feel a little sharp at first. But there is so much peace in the unadorned.

The black coffee rule is about refusing to mask the reality of your life. If a day is quiet and unremarkable, let it be. If you are tired, let yourself be tired without performing productivity. If you are happy, let yourself be happy without needing to document it.

I'm learning to take my life black. Simple, potent, and exactly as it is. No additions required.

June · Entry Three
There Is a Difference Between Fighting for Love and Forcing It
Fighting for love still has love in it. Forcing it only has fear.

I used to think fighting for love meant holding on no matter how much it hurt. But now I think there is a difference between fighting for love and forcing it. And sometimes, we only learn the difference after we have already lost so much of ourselves trying.

When you are fighting for love, you are not the only one starting the hard conversations. You are not the only one apologizing, adjusting, trying to understand. There is effort from both sides. But when you are forcing it, everything starts to feel one-sided.

You become the one who always brings things up. The one who notices the distance. The one who asks, "Are we okay?" And slowly, you start confusing crumbs with effort — feeling grateful for the smallest attention because you have been emotionally hungry for so long.

Fighting for love brings repair. Forcing it brings the same wound in different versions. Love can be hard, yes. But it should not constantly feel like you are trying to open a door someone keeps closing.

If you are the only one fighting for it, maybe you are not fighting for love anymore. Maybe you are forcing it.

June · Entry Four
5 Signs You're Emotionally Tired From Trying Too Hard
You are not asking for too much. You are just tired of asking the wrong person.

There is a kind of tiredness that does not look obvious. You still wake up. You still reply to messages. You still do what you have to do. But inside, something feels heavy. Not broken. Just tired.

You no longer want to explain yourself. You have said it already — in different versions, softly, carefully, even when you were hurting. When nothing really changes, silence starts to feel easier than repeating yourself.

You feel guilty for needing basic things. You are not asking for luxury. You are asking for honesty, consistency, effort. But when you ask the wrong person, even the bare minimum starts to feel like too much.

Small things make you emotional. It is not the small thing that hurts. It is the history behind it. All the times you stayed quiet. All the times you told yourself not to make it a big deal.

You miss who you were before this feeling. The version who did not overthink every reply. The version who still believed love should feel soft.

You are holding on because of how it used to be. Waiting for someone's old version can slowly make you lose your own. And maybe the most honest question is: "Do I still feel like myself while loving them?"

June · Entry Five
What Peace Taught Me About Letting Go
Sometimes letting go is not losing someone. Sometimes it is finally getting yourself back.

I used to think letting go would feel like one big decision. Like one day I would wake up, feel nothing, and finally be ready. But it did not happen that way.

For me, letting go happened slowly. It happened in the moments I stopped checking. In the messages I no longer typed. In the explanations I no longer gave. In the silence I finally stopped trying to fill.

Peace taught me that not everything needs a final conversation. Some things already ended in the way they made you feel. The distance was already an answer. The inconsistency was already an answer. I just kept waiting for words because I did not want to accept the actions.

Letting go did not make me cold. It made me honest. Honest about what I needed. Honest about what I kept accepting. Honest about the way I was abandoning myself just to keep something alive.

Some things leave space when they leave. And maybe that space is not punishment. Maybe it is room — to breathe, to heal, to become someone who no longer begs to be chosen.
